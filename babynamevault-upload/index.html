How this is organized

Every name in BabyNameVault is grouped three ways: by culture, by style, and by gender. Here's exactly what each group means.

The 3 styles

Traditional

The established, classic form of a name as it's been spelled for generations within that culture.

Modern Trendy Respelling

A popular, phonetic twist on a familiar name that's common in recent birth records (e.g. Madison to Madisyn).

Distinctive & Inventive

More unique, standout spellings and invented forms that push further from the traditional version.

The 11 cultures

African

Naming traditions from across the African continent - Yoruba, Igbo, Swahili, Akan, Zulu and more - not tied to the U.S.

African American

Contemporary Black American naming traditions, including classic names and the modern inventive-spelling tradition distinct to the U.S.

Arabic

Names rooted in Arabic language and naming conventions, shown with native Arabic script alongside the Latin transliteration.

East Asian

Names from Chinese, Japanese and Korean naming traditions, shown with native script and common romanization variants.

Indian / South Asian

Names from Hindi and other South Asian languages, shown with Devanagari script and transliteration variants.

Irish / Celtic

Names rooted in Irish Gaelic, shown alongside the common English phonetic spelling parents often use.

Jewish / Hebrew

Names rooted in Hebrew and Jewish tradition, shown with Hebrew script and transliteration variants.

Latino / Hispanic

Names from Spanish-language naming traditions across Latin America and Spain, shown with accented native-spelling variants.

Pacific Islander / Hawaiian

Names from Hawaiian and other Pacific Islander traditions, including Samoan, Tongan, Fijian and Maori naming customs.

Southeast Asian

Names from Vietnamese, Thai, Indonesian, Filipino and other Southeast Asian naming traditions.

Western / European

Names rooted in English and broader European naming traditions - the names most familiar in the U.S. mainstream.

Each name also shows every real spelling variant side by side, and native-script names include the original script wherever one exists - so you see exactly how a name looks and sounds in its own language, not just an anglicized guess.
